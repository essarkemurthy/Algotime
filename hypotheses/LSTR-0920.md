# Hypothesis LSTR-0920 — NIFTY long ATM straddle, intraday, no directional view

Status: DRAFT (not approved; nothing may be run until the owner approves this file)

Family: NIFTY intraday long premium. Variants tried in family: 0.

## Economic reason
Bhat, Pandey & Nageswara Rao (*J. Futures Markets* 2024, NIFTY 2017-2020) and Muravyev & Ni (*JFE* 2020,
SPX) find that option sellers earn the variance risk premium **overnight** and give part of it back
**intraday**: short option returns are positive at night and negative during the day. The mirror image is
that a long, delta-neutral option position has positive expected return during trading hours. The
proposed reason is that sellers demand compensation for the gap risk they carry while markets are closed,
and that intraday realised variance tends to exceed what the morning premium implies once overnight
decay has been paid. The other side is the overnight premium seller who keeps the position open into the
day; they lose intraday on average and are paid for it at night.

This is the opposite of what the current engine does (buy one leg on a directional signal). It needs no
signal, no volume, and no view on direction, so it also tests whether our option data and fills are
good enough to see a documented effect at all.

## Exact rules
- Instrument: NIFTY 50 weekly options on NSE (NFO). One lot per leg (65 from the Jan-2026 series; the
  historical lot size is irrelevant because R is defined per unit and P&L is scaled to today's lot).
- Session: every NSE trading day. Bar size: 1 minute (Breeze `get_historical_data_v2`, options).
- Expiry: the nearest listed weekly expiry with **DTE ≥ 1** (on an expiry day, use the following week).
  Expiry calendar: Thursday through 28-Aug-2025, Tuesday from the 2-Sep-2025 series; a holiday moves
  it to the previous trading day. The fetch script tries the scheduled day then the previous trading day.
- Strike: the 50-point strike nearest to the NIFTY spot at the 09:20 bar open (day t uses only the
  09:20 bar of day t; no earlier data is needed).
- Entry: buy 1 lot of the ATM call and 1 lot of the ATM put at the **09:20 bar open** of each leg.
- Exit: sell both legs at the **15:20 bar open**. No stop, no target, no trailing rule: the maximum loss
  is the premium paid, and any intraday stop would be a tuning choice the hypothesis does not make.
- If either leg has no 09:20 bar or no 15:20 bar, the day is skipped and counted in the funnel.
- R for the day = (exit straddle value − entry straddle value − costs) / entry straddle value.
  So R = −1 is a total loss of premium; R = +0.10 is a 10% gain on premium.
- Skip rules: none beyond missing data. Budget day, RBI policy days and expiry weeks are reported as
  slices, never filtered.

## Parameters
| name | value | why this value | range to examine in walk-forward |
|---|---|---|---|
| entry time | 09:20 | after the first 5 minutes' spread blow-out; same convention as the retail 920 straddle so results are comparable | 09:17, 09:20, 09:30 (robustness only) |
| exit time | 15:20 | before closing-session illiquidity; keeps the position strictly intraday | 15:10, 15:20, 15:25 |
| min DTE | 1 | avoids 0DTE gamma/theta extremes the paper does not describe | 1, 2 |
| strike | nearest 50 | ATM is where delta ≈ 0 for the pair | fixed |
| lots | 1 per leg | sizing is not part of the hypothesis | fixed |

The grid above is 3 × 3 × 2 = 18 cells and exists only for gate 2 (robustness). It is not used to pick
parameters.

## Data
- Test set: NIFTY option 1-minute bars, ATM CE and PE of the chosen expiry, every trading day from the
  earliest date Breeze serves (to be established by a probe; the probe result goes in this file before the
  run) through 2026-11-19. Not consumed by any family in `ledger.md`.
- Fetch budget: 2 contracts × 1 session per day ≈ 2 Breeze calls per trading day; 3 years ≈ 1,500 calls,
  within one day's 4,500 quota. Must run inside the app's Breeze session or with the app stopped
  (single-session API).
- Cost model (per straddle-day, 1 lot each leg, in rupees, converted to R by the day's premium):
  brokerage ₹20 × 4 orders = ₹80; STT 0.1% of sell-side premium; exchange + SEBI + stamp ≈ 0.06% of
  turnover; GST 18% on brokerage and exchange charges; slippage ₹1.00 per leg per side × 65 = ₹260.
  Base case ≈ ₹375 on a ₹20,000 straddle (≈ 1.9% of premium). Robust case doubles slippage.
- Walk-forward: 12 months in-sample / 3 months out-of-sample, rolling, as in `strategies/backtest.py`.

## Bar to clear
Standard gates from `strategies/backtest.py --gates`, restated for this family:
1. Walk-forward OOS, base costs: n ≥ 100 days, mean R ≥ 0.10 is **not** appropriate for a premium-return
   series (a 10% average daily gain on premium would be absurd); the gate for this family is mean R ≥ 0.02
   with bootstrap 95% CI lower bound > 0 + 0.02 × 0 (variants tried = 0).
2. Robustness: mean R > 0 under the robust cost case; ≥ 60% of the 18 grid cells positive; walk-forward
   picks stable parameters.
3. OOS max drawdown ≤ 12 R.
4. Paper phase: ≥ 30 sessions with the same rules in the paper engine, realised R within tolerance of
   backtest R.
5. Live: one lot per leg for ≥ 20 sessions before any size change, and only after the account can carry
   it (≈ ₹20,000 premium per straddle at today's levels; usable capital is ₹7,900).

If any gate fails, the family is frozen at variants = 1 and this file records FAIL. No stop-loss variant,
no time-of-day variant and no DTE variant may be validated on the same data.

## Pre-registered slices (reported, Holm-adjusted, never filters)
year; DTE bucket (1, 2-4, ≥5); expiry weekday regime (Thursday era vs Tuesday era); India VIX tercile
at 09:20 (from the `candles` table if present, else omitted); budget day and RBI policy days; days with
|overnight gap| > 0.5%; day-of-week.

## Known deviations from the source papers
- The papers measure delta-hedged returns; this tests an unhedged ATM straddle, which is delta-neutral
  at entry only. Intraday drift adds directional noise but no bias if the entry is unconditional.
- The papers' sample ends in 2020; weekly expiries, lot sizes and participation have changed since. That
  is exactly why the result must be re-measured.
